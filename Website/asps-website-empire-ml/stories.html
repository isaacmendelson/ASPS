Voice cloning

I thought it was my son calling. The voice sounded exactly like him — his tone, his words, everything. He said he was in trouble and needed money urgently. I was about to transfer the money when my real son walked through the door. It was an AI-generated voice scam. I still can't believe how real it sounded.

👩

Parent, 62 years old
Bank impersonation

They called from what appeared to be my bank's official number. They knew my name, my account details, everything. They said there was suspicious activity and I needed to verify my identity. I'm a software engineer — I thought I knew better. But the pressure, the urgency, the professionalism... I gave them my codes.

👨

Anonymous

Software engineer, 34 years old
Romance scam

We talked for months. He was kind, understanding, everything I was looking for. He was working abroad, he said. When he needed money for an emergency, I didn't hesitate. Then another emergency. And another. By the time I realized, I had lost my savings. But worse than the money was the betrayal of trust.

👩

Anonymous

Teacher, 45 years old
Tech support

A popup on my computer said I had a virus. It looked exactly like a Windows alert. I called the number and they walked me through "fixing" the problem — which meant giving them remote access to my computer. They were very professional. My daughter, who works in IT, stopped it just in time.

👴

Breaking the stigma

Why we share these stories

Scams thrive on silence and shame. When people don't talk about their experiences, scammers win. By sharing stories openly, we break the cycle.

  • ✓ Awareness prevents victims. Every story shared helps someone recognize a scam before it's too late.
  • ✓ Shame protects scammers. When victims stay silent, scammers can keep using the same tactics.
  • ✓ Anyone can be targeted. Doctors, lawyers, engineers, and cybersecurity experts have all fallen victim.
  • ✓ Community heals. Knowing you're not alone helps survivors recover and move forward.
