Five commitments, written down.

Updated quarterly · open issues tracked publicly

01

Every alert is multi-channel.

An alert is never just a colour, never just a tone, never just a haptic. Every alert ships visual + audible + haptic + plain-language text. If you can perceive any one of those four, you can perceive the alert.

ProofTested across 7 channels, including silent-mode and watch-only.

02

Speech-first, touch-second.

You can complete every primary task — accept, decline, mute, request human help — by voice alone. We test with VoiceOver and TalkBack on every release, not just before audits.

ProofScreen-reader users complete 100% of critical paths in our test suite.

03

Readable at 200% zoom.

No text under 16px in primary surfaces. No fixed heights that cut off zoomed content. Line lengths stay between 45–75 characters at any setting. Reflow tested down to 320px.

ProofZero zoom-related defects in last three audit reports.

04

Plain language, not legal language.

Every alert is rewritten by a copywriter, not a translator. Average reading age across in-app text: 11. We never put unexplained scores, percentages or technical model names in front of a person making a decision under stress.

ProofHemingway grade 5–7 across 96% of alert strings.

05

Tested with the people we built it for.

Every release ships only after a usability run with disabled users — paid, recruited through partner organisations, not employees. We publish what we learned, not just that we tested.

Proof34 unique testers in 2025, paid above market rate.

How we test · Who we test with

The audit tells us what passes. The people tell us what works.

Automated tools catch about a third of accessibility defects. The rest only show up when somebody who actually relies on the technology tries to use it. So every release goes to a panel of paid testers before it goes to anyone else.

We compensate testers above market rate, in their currency, with no NDA on what they say publicly about us. If we get it wrong, the world should know.
