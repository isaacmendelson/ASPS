    AI voice cloning scams are accelerating globally

  2. Regulation May 8, 2026

    UK scam reimbursement rules are reshaping fraud prevention

    New UK reimbursement regulations are increasing pressure on financial institutions to prevent scams before losses occur. The focus is shifting away from post-fraud reimbursement and toward proactive, real-time intervention at the moment of decision.

    Source · ThreatMark
  3. Research May 6, 2026

    Even experts struggle to detect AI-generated scam voices

    A recent peer-reviewed study found that participants — including those highly confident in their abilities — often failed to distinguish real human speech from AI-generated voice clones. Human intuition alone is becoming insufficient.

    Source · Academic Research (ArXiv)
  4. Policy May 5, 2026

    GASA calls for stronger global anti-scam coordination

    The Global Anti-Scam Alliance's standing policy agenda continues to emphasise consumer education, intelligence sharing, cross-sector collaboration and coordinated anti-scam initiatives across borders — themes that have only intensified through 2025–2026.

    Source · GASA Policy Agenda
  5. Alert May 3, 2026

    AI scam threats are becoming industrialised at scale

    Industry researchers warn that AI-powered scams are becoming scalable, automated and globally distributed, dramatically increasing the speed and volume of attacks against consumers — and shortening the window in which a person can react.

    Source · GASA Report
  6. Regulation April 28, 2026

    Governments increase focus on AI safety and fraud regulation

    Governments and regulators worldwide are beginning to introduce frameworks focused on AI-generated deception, impersonation and consumer fraud protection. Responsible AI is becoming a regulatory priority — not a discussion topic.

    Source · Eversheds Sutherland

    Bank impersonation scams surge in European markets

    Financial institutions across Europe report a significant increase in sophisticated impersonation attacks targeting customers through phone calls and text messages — with displaced losses landing on banks under emerging reimbursement rules.
