How it feels

Three moments, one quiet shoulder.

Three real-world situations, and what ASPS does in each — written the way they actually happen, not the way they look in brochures.

Tuesday · 14:42

"This is the bank's fraud team."

The voice is calm, official, and knows your last four digits. They need you to move money to "a safe account" — right now.

ASPS reads the voice (flagged as synthetic), checks the number (not your bank's), and the script (matches known impersonation patterns). The verdict lands on screen before they finish the sentence.

Saturday · 21:08

A text from your "son."

New number. "Hi Dad, my phone broke, can you send €600 to this account by tomorrow morning, please don't tell Mum."

ASPS recognises the cadence — a templated message pattern that's been circulating widely this month. It surfaces the warning, the alternate verification path, and a one-tap call to your actual son's known number.

Sunday · 11:14

Mum is on the phone — except she isn't.

Her voice. Her cadence. Crying. "I've had an accident, please don't tell Dad, I just need money for the lawyer."

You get a quiet alert on your phone too — the family layer fired. Voice profile doesn't match her usual baseline. You call her real number while still listening. She picks up. She's fine.

Questions people ask

Honest answers to the things that matter.

  • Does ASPS listen to all my phone calls?

    It analyses audio on your device while a call is active — looking for synthetic-voice signatures, impersonation patterns and known scam scripts. The audio itself is not stored or sent to our servers. Verdicts and patterns are; the content of your conversation isn't.

  • Will it block my real bank when they call?

    No. ASPS doesn't block calls — it shows you what it sees and lets you decide. When a number genuinely belongs to your bank, that's part of the signal, not against it. You always have the final say.

  • Can I set this up for a parent who isn't tech-savvy?

    Yes — the Family plan is built for exactly that. You install once, your relative confirms on their device (consent is required), and after that the interface is reduced to the simplest possible alert. They never have to interpret a dashboard.

  • What happens to the data ASPS sees?

    Pattern data and verdicts are kept; raw audio and message bodies aren't. Anything that does leave your device is encrypted, region-pinned, and never sold. The full data policy is on the privacy page.

  • Does it work on iPhone, Android, or both?

    Both. Some channels (calls especially) integrate more deeply on Android due to platform limits on iOS — the differences are listed transparently when you set up each device.

  • Can I cancel any time?

    Yes — monthly billing, cancel from the app, no exit fees, no "win-back" calls.

The point isn't catching scams. The point is the next thirty seconds — what you do, before money or trust changes hands.
