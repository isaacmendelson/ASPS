01 / The basics

What ASPS is, and isn't.

The questions everyone asks first — what we do, what we don't, and how it differs from things you may already have.

  • What is ASPS, in one sentence?

    A real-time layer that watches the calls, messages, emails and web pages reaching you, and warns you — in plain language — when something looks like a scam. It doesn't decide for you; it gives you the seconds back to decide for yourself.

  • How is this different from my antivirus or my bank's app?

    Antivirus protects your device from malware. Banking apps protect your account from unauthorised access. ASPS protects you — the person being talked into a decision — across every channel a scammer can reach you on. The three are complementary; ASPS sits in the gap the other two were never built to cover.

  • Does it block calls and messages automatically?

    No. We deliberately don't auto-block. The platform shows you what it sees and lets you decide. We hold to this even when it would be easier to flip a switch on your behalf — judgement belongs to the person on the line.

  • Will it slow my phone down?

    The on-device detectors run on the same neural chips your phone already uses for photos and dictation. Typical battery impact is <2% per day. If you notice anything, support will help us tune it.

  • What languages does ASPS work in?

    Real-time analysis is fully supported in English, Hebrew, French, Spanish and Russian — the five languages of our consumer launch. Partial detection is available in another 14 languages; accuracy levels are listed on the platform page.

  • Is ASPS available where I live?

    Direct-to-consumer plans are live in the EU, UK and Israel. Enterprise deployments (via banks, telcos and insurers) reach further. The full availability map is on the individuals page.

02 / Protection

What it actually catches.

Channel by channel, attack by attack — what triggers an alert and what doesn't.

  • How does the voice-clone detection work?

    The audio stream of an active call is analysed on-device for synthetic-voice signatures — the artefacts that current voice models leave in spectral and temporal patterns. The verdict is a percentage, refreshed every few seconds, with a short explanation of what triggered it.

  • Will it catch a deepfake of someone I know?

    In the vast majority of cases, yes — current voice clones leave artefacts that real human voices don't. We also compare against a baseline of how your contacts have sounded on previous calls. No detector is ever 100%; that's why ASPS surfaces the verdict but leaves the decision to you.

  • What about scams that use no voice at all — just text?

    SMS, WhatsApp, Telegram, iMessage and email are screened for known phishing patterns, sender spoofing, urgency-pressure language and dangerous links. Browser layer catches the click before it lands.

  • Does it work on encrypted messengers like Signal or WhatsApp?

    On Android, yes — the analysis happens after decryption on your device, so end-to-end encryption isn't broken. On iOS, coverage is more limited due to platform restrictions; SMS, mail and browser are fully covered, with messengers handled through opt-in keyboard integration.

  • What kinds of bank scams does it catch?

    Bank-impersonation calls (the "fraud team" script), authorised-push-payment scams ("move it to a safe account"), CFO/CEO fraud at work, and the SMS variants of all of the above. Coverage is strongest where partners are integrated, but baseline screening works on every account.

  • Will it flag my actual bank when they call?

    No — when a number genuinely matches your bank's verified line and the script doesn't match a scam pattern, that's part of the green signal, not against it. False positives are rare and tuned weekly.

  • What about romance scams or long-running cons?

    The text and email layers track conversation patterns over time, flagging the typical financial-ask escalation. We don't read the romance for you — we just notice when the script tips into the part that has worked on thousands of people before.

  • Can it stop a scam that's already in progress?

    Yes — alerts run live during calls and as messages arrive. Many of the saves in our stories archive happened mid-call, with the alert landing inside the first 30 seconds.

03 / Setup

Getting started in five minutes.

Install, permissions, devices and the practical bits.

  • How long does setup actually take?

    About four minutes for an individual plan, eight for a family. The install includes a one-time baseline of your typical call partners so the voice-match works from day one.

  • What permissions does the app need?

    Call audio (for voice detection), notifications (for alerts), SMS/messaging access (for text screening), and optionally browser/keyboard integration. Each permission is explained on its own screen with what it does and what happens if you skip it.

  • Does it work on iPhone and Android?

    Both. Android gets the deepest call integration; iOS gets full SMS, mail and browser coverage plus opt-in messenger keyboards. Differences are listed on each setup screen — no surprises mid-install.

  • Can I install it on my parent's phone for them?

    You can set up the Family plan from your phone, but your parent's device requires their own consent step before any protection activates. We won't install anything silently on a phone that isn't yours, ever.

  • Does ASPS work on a desktop computer?

    The browser layer runs as a desktop extension on Chrome, Firefox, Safari and Edge — catching phishing pages, fake bank logins and malicious downloads. Phone-call detection requires the phone app.

  • How do I uninstall, if I want to?

    Settings → Account → Remove ASPS. Everything stored on your device is deleted on the spot; anything on our servers is purged within 30 days. No retention by default, no exit fees.

04 / Family

Protecting people you worry about.

The questions families ask about setting this up for parents, kids, partners and grandparents.

  • How does the family-loop alert work?

    When an opted-in family member's phone receives an ASPS alert during a high-risk call, a parallel notification reaches whichever family contact they've nominated. So if "Mum" gets called by a voice clone of her son, the real son's phone lights up too.

  • Is this surveillance? Can I see who my parent talks to?

    No, and no. The family-loop only fires on high-risk events with confirmed scam signals. We don't share call lists, message bodies, contacts or any normal communication. The whole design is opt-in consent first, with the loop visible to both sides at all times.

  • How many family members can be on one plan?

    Up to six. They can be in different countries, on different platforms, with different language settings.

  • What is the "safe word" feature?

    A private word your family agrees on, known only to humans on the line. If a voice claims to be a family member and is asked for it, real family knows the word; clones don't. ASPS prompts you when this would be the right moment.

  • My parent isn't tech-comfortable — is the UI hard?

    Family plans include "easy mode" — a stripped-down interface that shows alerts as a single clear screen with two big buttons (continue / end call) and nothing else. No dashboards, no settings to find.

05 / Privacy & data

What we see, what we don't keep.

The questions our trust team writes the careful answers to. The full data policy lives on the privacy page.

  • Does ASPS listen to and store all my calls?

    Audio is analysed on your device while a call is active and is not stored or transmitted to our servers. The verdict and pattern data is; the audio content of your conversations isn't. Same for SMS and message bodies — the content stays local.

  • Where is my data stored?

    EU users: in the EU. UK users: in the UK. Israeli users: in Israel. Region-pinned, with no cross-region transfers without an active legal hold. The full residency map is on the privacy page.

  • Do you sell behavioural data to advertisers or partners?

    No. Never. This is written into our terms, not a marketing promise — and one of the five public principles on the about page.

  • What if a government or court asks for my data?

    We respond only to lawfully valid requests in the relevant jurisdiction, push back on overbroad ones, and publish a transparency report twice a year. Because most of your content never leaves your device, there isn't much to hand over even if compelled.

  • Are the AI models audited?

    Yes. Annual third-party audits cover model accuracy, bias by language and region, and behaviour on edge cases. Findings are published, not buried. Model cards for each production detector live on the platform page.

  • Can I export everything you have on me?

    Yes. Settings → Account → Export. You get a portable JSON file within 24 hours, covering every piece of data tied to your account. Free, unlimited, no friction.

  • Can I delete everything you have on me?

    Yes. Same screen. Deletion is irreversible and propagates to all backups within 30 days. You'll get a confirmation email when the last copy is gone.

07 / If something went wrong

Help, when you actually need it.

If you or someone you love just got scammed — start here. If you're not sure, also start here.

  • I think I just got scammed. What do I do — right now?

    Call your bank's verified line (the number on the back of your card) immediately. Change your email password. File a report. We've written the full short version on the support page — none of it requires ASPS, it's the steps regardless.

  • ASPS didn't catch a scam I encountered. What happens now?

    Send us the details via the scam tip route. We pay attention to misses. Every one becomes a model-improvement task with a name attached. Where the miss directly cost you money, our incident team reviews whether our service-level commitment applies.

  • I got an alert and I think it was wrong — a real friend, real call.

    Tap "this was fine" on the alert screen. The verdict is logged as a false positive against that voice/number, and the model learns. It happens; we'd rather a false positive than a missed clone.

  • Someone is using ASPS branding in a scam against me.

    Report it immediately to security@asps.io. We've never cold-called or cold-texted any user. Anyone claiming to be us, who you didn't contact first, is not us.

  • How do I report a vulnerability you should know about?

    Responsible disclosure programme details on the contact page. PGP key, scope, bounty bands — all public.
